02 · The idea

One circuit. Two energy problems.

Electrons

NH₃ geometry → electronic Hamiltonian → two qubits → VQE ground energy.

Check: compare with exact diagonalization of the same reduced model.

Vibrations

Optimized geometry → six modes → stretch potential → vibrational Hamiltonian → VQE zero-point energy.

Then: E₁ − E₀ gives the fundamental transition; excited levels here come from classical diagonalization.

The geometry, frequencies and potential come from classical RHF/STO-3G. VQE uses a noiseless simulated quantum circuit. This is a validated teaching workflow, with no demonstrated speed advantage.

ZPE: E₀ above the potential minimum. Fundamental: (E₁ − E₀)/(hc), about 3804.1 cm⁻¹ in the larger anharmonic model versus 3831.8 cm⁻¹ harmonically. The four-state model gives about 3813.9 cm⁻¹. Its ground state is reliable relative to the larger basis, while its higher levels are less converged.

The one-mode ZPE is about 22.90 kJ/mol. It is not the molecule’s total six-mode ZPE. A harmonic six-mode estimate from the rounded frequencies above is about 8804.45 cm⁻¹, or 105.32 kJ/mol; this is a derived classical estimate, not a six-mode VQE result.

Saved results differ across the submitted files

The Word report quotes an electronic VQE total of −55.4536353383 Ha and a vibrational error of 0.0027 cm⁻¹. The notebook’s electronic optimizer output instead gives −55.4536348276 Ha. Its vibrational optimizer cell reports best warm-start seed 7, 1913.9526 cm⁻¹, with an error of 0.0035 cm⁻¹ against 1913.9490 cm⁻¹; a later validation cell reports a rounded zero error. Those saved outputs are not mutually consistent. The historical tables use the explicit optimizer output. The interactive explorer uses a separate reproduction of the geometry, Hessian and scan with full-precision data and a 1000-evaluation COBYLA ground-state search using analytically evaluated circuit amplitudes. This is a noiseless classical simulation of the same circuit, not an additional hardware experiment. The reproduction is labeled separately and does not replace the submission. Understand the energy picture and validation

Harmonic first

Near equilibrium, the potential resembles a bowl: V(ξ) = ½hcν̃ξ². Quantizing this oscillator gives Eₙ = hcν̃(n + ½). The ground level sits above the bottom even at zero temperature. Purple probability curves show where the coordinate is likely to be measured; they are stationary for an energy eigenstate.

Then include anharmonicity

The scanned curve is steeper under compression. Solving kinetic plus fitted potential mixes oscillator basis states and produces uneven level spacings. The plotted anharmonic probabilities use exact classical eigenvectors of the full-precision reproduced four-state matrix, or of the imported calculation. Only the ground energy was obtained by VQE; excited levels here are classical reference results.

Methods, units, and validation

Dimensionless normal coordinate ξ = √(ω/ℏ) Q. Potential and levels are shown as E/(hc) in cm⁻¹, relative to the optimized RHF minimum. Conversion: 1 cm⁻¹ ≈ 0.01196266 kJ/mol. A classical oscillator may have arbitrary energy; using Eₙ here allows a visual comparison at equal energy.

Checks in the submitted notebook cover Hermiticity, circuit/matrix expectation agreement, variational bound, curvature/Hessian agreement, and a four-state versus larger-basis comparison. The polynomial’s small fit residual checks its agreement with sampled RHF points; it does not establish global accuracy outside the scan. The reported 60-state calculation is a larger-basis reference, not a full domain-convergence proof. The umbrella mode is shown locally and harmonically, not as a computed inversion double well.

04 · Next steps

What could we learn next?

Toward amine chemistry

Methylamine → protonation thermodynamics → drug-relevant fragments. Larger models, solvent effects and validation are needed.

From levels to thermodynamics
